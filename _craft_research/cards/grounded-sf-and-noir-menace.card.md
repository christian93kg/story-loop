# Card — grounded-sf-and-noir-menace
Applies to institutional/bureaucratic threat scenes and hardboiled-noir narration (the *Andor* / *Edgerunners* register) — power that operates through process, not shouting; menace built from calm surfaces over a known danger, not from spectacle.

## Tests
- Could the antagonist's line be read aloud as a memo, diagnosis, or procedure? If it needs a raised voice or an explanation of cruelty, flatten it.
- Is the worst thing in the scene delivered as an administrative action (reassignment, scheduling, paperwork) rather than a declared threat?
- Count anticipation+aftermath lines vs. lines depicting the violent act itself — if depiction wins, rebalance toward the former.
- Does the protagonist get shorter/flatter as pressure rises, or wittier? Wittier fails.
- Does a character announce their code, virtue, or cleverness, or is it only enacted? Announced fails.
- Does the setting description reveal *who* is seeing it (a POV judgment on the place), or could any narrator have written the same lines about it? Interchangeable fails.
- Does a sentence exist only to inform the reader of backstory, or is the history enacted through present behavior? Info-dump fails.
- Does violence, once it lands, run brief and unglamorized, or does the prose linger on it for cool? Lingering fails — the anticipation should have done the work.

## Kill list
- Villain who monologues, shouts, or explains why the cruelty is justified
- Punishment staged as declared violence instead of routed through paperwork, reassignment, or scheduling
- The violent act shown in extended detail instead of withheld and reported flat afterward
- Quips or one-liners built to make the reader admire the narrator
- Stated virtue/competence ("I don't take dirty money, that's just who I am")
- Backstory delivered as an explanatory paragraph instead of a behavioral tell
- Static room inventory not filtered through motion or the POV character's judgment
- Lingering, glamorized violence once the act finally lands

## Micro-examples
Institutional threat, declared → procedural:
"The officer threatened to destroy her if she didn't talk."
→ "'We treat this early,' he said. 'Or we treat it later. Later is harder on everyone.'"

Interrogation under rising pressure, witty → compressed:
A three-sentence witty retort under interrogation.
→ "Where were you?" / "Out." / "Doing what?" / "Walking."
