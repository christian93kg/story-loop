# Card — voice-and-dialect
Applies whenever a character speaks English as a second language, or in a class/regional register distinct from the narrator's — any language pairing, not just Romanian.

## Tests
- Tense flattening (Mandarin-type L1): bare verb stems, missing -ed/-s, no respelling — does tense marking drop instead of a word getting misspelled?
- Word order transfer: does topic-fronting or verb-final order carry origin ("This job, very difficult") with no misspelling?
- False friends (educated Latin-L1): does a real cognate error (assist/attend, actually/currently) make the line sound fluent-but-wrong, not broken?
- Calqued idiom: is the foreignness a literally-translated idiom/proverb, not altered spelling?
- Untranslated culture-word: does it carry social weight English lacks (kinship term, honorific), glossed once by context/apposition — not a decorative synonym?
- Register collision: does one bicultural speaker's line span at least two registers (bookish + street/code-switch) in a single breath?
- Same-L1 differentiation: is class/education carried by Latinate over-reach vs. idiomatic bluntness, holding the L1-transfer markers constant between the two speakers?
- Consistency: cap 2-3 markers per voice, chosen for that character's actual L1 — not a grab-bag dialect costume.

## Kill list
- Any phonetic respelling ("ze," "vent," "dat," "gonna" as a marked feature) — if correcting the spelling kills the distinctiveness, it was eye-dialect.
- Dropped-letter apostrophes ("huntin'," "'ello") as an accent marker.
- Catchphrases or a repeated physical/verbal tic standing in for voice.
- Decorative untranslated words that have a plain English equivalent (exoticizing, not anchoring).
- Mixed L1-transfer patterns in one character without biographical justification (reads as authorial carelessness).
- Constant syntactic degradation regardless of mood — no stress curve, it's a gimmick.
- Broken grammar with no compensating intelligence marker (precise/lyrical word choice) — reads as childlike or comic instead of dignified.

## Micro-examples
Before (eye-dialect): "I weel assist ze meeting, no problem."
After (false friend, Spanish L1): "I will assist the meeting at three." (means *attend*) / "Actually I live in Madrid." (means *currently*)

Before (flat, no origin signal): "The fish here is very fresh."
After (Mandarin topic-fronting): "This restaurant, the fish very fresh."
After (calqued idiom, Hemingway-style Spanish): "What passes that you are so sad?"

### Digest — voice-and-dialect
When: any speaker whose first language or class/regional register differs from the narrator's.
- Origin rides syntax only: tense flattening, topic-fronting, calqued idiom, real false-friend errors.
- Cap 2–3 markers per voice, chosen for that character's actual first language; syntax degrades under stress, recovers after.
- Untranslated culture-words carry weight English lacks, glossed once by context — never decoration.
- Broken grammar pairs with a compensating intelligence marker (precise or lyrical word choice) — dignified, not comic.
- Same-origin speakers split by diction (Latinate over-reach vs. idiomatic bluntness), transfer markers constant.
KILL: dropped-letter apostrophes; catchphrase tics; grab-bag dialect costumes; constant degradation with no stress curve.
