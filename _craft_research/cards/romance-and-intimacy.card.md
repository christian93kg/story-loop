# Card — romance-and-intimacy
Applies to: any tenderness, attraction, or relationship-development beat — first touch, confession, sex, reunion, grief over a loved one.

## Tests
- One exact physical particular per beat, or only abstraction? Replace each abstraction with a body in space (a belt buckle, a hand, a distance closed).
- Where does the meaningful exchange actually happen — approach, act, or aftermath? Cut everywhere else; keep only that location.
- Does a fade/cut land on a changed situation? If the aftermath looks identical to the before, the cut was evasion, not craft.
- Does at least one party want contradictory things, or hold less power than the other? If both are serenely sure and equal, the scene is idealized.
- Is a grand claim about the other person anchored in one concrete sense AND slightly undercut? Pure exaltation reads as worship.
- After a loss, is it rendered in one object/action and then stopped? If still narrating the feeling three sentences later, cut sooner.
- Is POV held rigidly inside one consciousness, reporting only what that specific person would notice? Drifting to omniscient noticing = generic.
- Does the scene do work beyond itself — shift power, exchange information, or create something to lose? A tender interlude that only admires the couple is decorative.
- Is any figurative language anchored in an exact physical action (a hand, a grip, a fabric), or does it float free of the body? Floating metaphor reads as purple.

## Kill list
- Abstraction/euphemism standing in for the body: "souls merging," "electricity," "completeness," "front parlour."
- Narrator certifying the moment: "it was perfect," "she had never felt so alive."
- Fade-to-black used to dodge the emotional work rather than relocate it to the aftermath.
- Two people serenely, uncomplicatedly sure of each other with no cost, doubt, or asymmetry.
- Beat-sheet romance-genre declarations swappable between any couple: "my love," "my everything," "I've never met anyone like you."
- Extended narrated grief that keeps explaining the feeling instead of cutting after one image.
- Choreography for its own sake — physical description with no emotional information attached to it.
- Over-explained characters with no "half-known" remainder; full comprehension of a love interest produces predictable, stereotyped beats.

## Micro-examples
**Logistics over abstraction:**
Before: "They became one, souls merging in waves of ecstasy."
After: "His belt buckle was cold; she felt it against her hip before his hand."

**Ambivalence over certainty:**
Before: "Being with him was perfect; she had no doubts at all."
After: "She wanted him and wanted to leave, and couldn't tell the two apart."
