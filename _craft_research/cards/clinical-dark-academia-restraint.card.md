# Card — clinical-dark-academia-restraint
Applies to: dread-and-restraint beats, amoral/cold-protagonist scenes, dark-academia institutional atmosphere, and any blood/body-horror rendered through euphemism.

## Tests
- At the scene's worst turn, does the narrator under-react? If narrator alarm matches reader alarm, you've done the reader's work for them — cut the reaction.
- Map what the reader knows vs. the protagonist knows vs. the institution knows, scene by scene. If all three match, the dread has collapsed — rebuild the gap.
- Does the protagonist observe a living person and a corpse/victim with the same syntax and attention? A register shift there narrates the coldness instead of embodying it.
- Does a paragraph explain how the magic/system *works* rather than how it feels to be near the people who wield it? Convert to a dramatized fragment or cut.
- Have you revealed the disturbing *fact* early and withheld only its *cause* and *feeling*? Hoarding the fact itself produces confusion, not dread.
- Scan for intensifying adverbs on violence ("brutally," "savagely") — replace with flat repeated plain action instead of raising volume.
- Beta-read for confusion about basic who/what/where. If readers are lost on fact (not meaning), you've over-withheld — restore concrete grounding.

## Kill list
- Simile or emotive adjective on a violent beat ("horrified," "bloom," "erupt")
- Any sentence stating the protagonist is cold/evil/monstrous rather than showing the gaze that proves it
- "Euphemistically called" or any editorial gloss on institutional euphemism
- Named sensation as verb-subject ("the hunger gnawed," "dread clawed")
- Magic-system/world-system exposition dumps (Accords, Houses, founding dates, lore)
- Adverb-stacked violence ("brutally, savagely, in a frenzied explosion")
- Narrator matching or exceeding the reader's horror in real time
- Tartt's ornate/purple adjectival surface — borrow her social-texture *method*, never her prose register

## Micro-examples
Before: "I was horrified, sickened to my core, as I realized what they truly intended to do to me."
After: "They explained what would be required of my body, and how many times, and I remember mostly that the room was cold and that I nodded."

Before: "The Vyrnar clan's blood-magic drew on the three Humours, bound by the Crimson Accord of 1612, governed by the Eight Houses…"
After: "They drank their wine cut with something darker and never offered you the bottle. They spoke a Latin you half-recognized. You learned to want the half you couldn't follow."

### Digest — clinical-dark-academia-restraint
When: dread-and-restraint beats, cold-protagonist scenes, institutional atmosphere, euphemized body-horror.
- Keep a knowledge gap open between reader, protagonist, and institution — all three matching collapses the dread.
- Reveal the disturbing fact early; withhold only its cause and its feeling.
- The cold gaze is embodied (same syntax for a living person and a corpse), never stated.
- Violence stays flat and plain — repeated ordinary action, no intensifying adverbs.
KILL: similes/emotive adjectives on violence; "he was monstrous" verdicts; editorial gloss on euphemism; sensation-as-subject ("the hunger gnawed"); lore dumps; Tartt's purple surface.
