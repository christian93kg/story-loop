# Card — openings-and-cold-open-craft
Applies to the first ~500 words of any story — the prologue and Chapter 1's opening beat. Read this card at new-story setup, before drafting the prologue.

## Tests
- Does something physically happen in the first sentence — a verb of action, not of being or thinking? First verb "was," "had been," or "thought" = fail.
- Is the protagonist's want visible in a physical action a camera could film within the first page? Want stated as an abstract noun ("survival," "revenge") = re-embody it.
- Is the strangest fact in the opening stated in the plainest possible sentence, with no intensifiers? Dressed in adjectives and exclamation = strip them.
- Delete every proper noun and plot fact from paragraph one — can a reader still describe the narrator's temperament? If not, the syntax isn't doing character work.
- Do the first 150 words contain a biographical résumé? Fail — "you" arrives through a present situation and a want, not a list of attributes.
- Does any sentence explain a term the narrator would never explain to themselves? Delete the explanation and trust the context.
- Is the world established by one telling object rather than adjectives ("grim," "polluted")? Replace the adjectives with the object.
- Do the first two sentences sit unmistakably in the target register? If the opening could be mistaken for cozy slice-of-life, the contract is broken.
- Retrospective open: does the first sentence reveal a consequence grim enough to make "how did that happen?" unavoidable? A vague promise ("everything changed") fails.
- Would the opening read identically in first person? Then the second person is decorative — rebuild it around what "you" are doing.

## Kill list
- Scenery-first throat-clearing (atmospheric before tense)
- The character sheet recited aloud (biography before situation)
- Exposition-first worldbuilding; the narrator stepping outside to explain
- Intensifier-dressed shock; world-by-adjective
- Abstract-noun stakes
- Decorative second person
- "Everything changed" / "little did you know" promises
- Waking-up, weather, and mirror openings

## Micro-examples
Before: "You are Sergeant Alia Voss, thirty-four, a decorated marksman with trust issues and a dead sister."
After: "You are not the kind of person who kneels in another woman's blood and checks her watch. But the watch is the mission, and you are already reaching for it."

Before: "The compound had been quiet for three days. Kesler checked his gear and thought about the mission ahead."
After: "The wire took the first man's throat before he'd finished the sentence, and Kesler was already moving."

Before: "It was the most horrifying, unbelievable thing I had ever witnessed — my own brother, changed beyond all recognition!"
After: "By the second week, my brother had stopped needing to eat, and none of us mentioned it."
