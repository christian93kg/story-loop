# Card — event-density-and-interiority-economy
Applies to every beat as the anti-bloat floor: run it on description, gesture, and reflection passages before shipping (spine's deletion/therefore-but/object-purpose/trigger checks already cover the core cut passes — the tests below are the additional, mode-specific checks).

## Tests
- Repeated-beat test: does this passage re-state an already-established emotional condition (more afraid, still afraid) without changing its terms? If yes, cut — a beat must alter, not elaborate.
- Room-function test: can you state in one phrase whether this room is currently a threat, a refuge, or a stage for the character's goal? If not, it isn't functioning — reframe or cut.
- One-detail test: if a description lists more than one or two sensory items, keep only the single detail that carries a judgment; cut the rest.
- Gesture test: could this gesture belong to any nervous/angry/sad person? If yes it's shorthand — replace the whole cluster with the one gesture only this character, this moment, would make (max one body-movement per emotional beat).
- Reflection-length test: is this feeling stated for more than two sentences without becoming action, perception, or dialogue? If so, convert it — reflection must run shorter than the event that caused it.
- Filter-word sweep: search for "you see / you notice / you feel / you realize"; delete the filter and present the thing directly unless the act of perceiving is itself the event.
- Density benchmark: if more than ~1 in 4 beats in a scene could be deleted without breaking the causal chain, the scene is chronological, not consequential — rebuild on therefore/but.

## Kill list
- Inventory description: objects listed with equal weight and no purpose (threat/refuge/tool)
- Gesture clusters: stacked stock tics (hand moved, eyes flicked, jaw tightened, fingers drummed)
- Free-floating reflection with no preceding external jolt
- Filter-word openers ("you notice that," "you see that," "you feel")
- Editorial character-verdicts stated outright ("he was a cruel man")
- Re-enacted emotional beats that add volume, not change
- Unfocused "crowding" — extra sensory detail that isn't in service of the central focus

## Micro-examples
Before: "She wore a blue dress with white dots, white organdy collar and cuffs, a straw hat with violets, white gloves, and a sachet at her throat."
After: "She had dressed so that anyone seeing her dead on the highway would know at once that she was a lady."

Before: "You notice that the door is open. You see that the lock has been forced. You feel cold air coming through."
After: "The door stands open; the lock hangs splintered from one screw; cold air moves across your ankles."
