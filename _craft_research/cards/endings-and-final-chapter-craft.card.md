# Card — endings-and-final-chapter-craft
Applies to the final act, last beats, and last line of a predetermined-ending run. Convention: add this mode to the story's House Register when the final act begins; remove it after the reading copy is filed.

## Tests
- Audit the promise ledger: every promise the early chapters made is paid on-page or deliberately marked open for a character/thematic reason. Unlisted, unaddressed promises fail.
- Name, in one sentence, what the protagonist permanently loses or forfeits to achieve the ending. If you cannot, the catharsis is gifted, not earned.
- If the protagonist gets their original want, the final scene shows the want's true cost or hollowness in a concrete image.
- Scan the last three sentences for emotion-naming words (grief, joy, devastated, free). Any present = telling the feeling instead of causing it.
- The final image is a concrete object or scene that appeared earlier, now changed; the ending repeats an earlier line/image with altered meaning traceable to a specific earlier instance.
- After the climax: no new characters, locations, or plot information — except inside a deliberately marked coda (a converging branch may earn a new location under that rule; unmarked additions fail).
- Late choices change *how* the pre-named ending is met, never *whether*. A late choice that can avert the named ending fails a destination-fixed story.
- When the narration takes the wheel in the finale, an in-world cause for the narrowed agency is visible on the page.
- The "you" is accounted for within the fiction by the final page; the finale never references reader, story, or author's hand.
- The coda type matches the story's thesis on meaning and order — a tidy epilogue on a chaos story lies; a hard cut on a story promising reckoning cheats. Weigh closure against the anti-closure camp: not every open question is owed an answer.

## Kill list
- Gifted catharsis / deus ex machina; the unearned epiphany
- Emotion-naming last lines
- False-ending fatigue — ending twice; new elements trickling in after the climax
- Meta-wink finales; everything-explained epilogues
- Late choices that avert the named ending; agency loss with no visible in-world cause
- The unresolved-"you" gimmick

## Micro-examples
Before: "She walked away, overwhelmed by a crushing grief she knew would never leave her."
After: "She walked away and it started to rain and she did not go back."

Before: "He found his brother alive. It was over. They went home."
After: "He found his brother alive. He would never tell him what he'd traded to the men at the border, or what he'd become in the trading. They went home."

The funnel, not the fork — at the climax the reader still chooses, but every branch converges on the pre-named ending: the choice determines *how* the character meets it, never *whether*.
