# Card — grit-and-momentum-cadence
Applies to physical-struggle beats in the underdog-fighter register: melee fights, grappling, any scene where the protagonist wins (or loses) through endurance rather than dominance.

## Tests
- On every solid hit, is the impact rendered in ≥2 fragments, each under five words, landing right at contact?
- Does every wind-up/approach run as one long subordinated sentence that then snaps to a short one on the blow? (accelerate, then brake — not all-short, not all-long)
- Does at least one injury from an earlier scene measurably slow, weaken, or redirect an action in this later one? An injury that's healed clean by the next scene is a reset, not a ledger.
- Read the fight aloud — do three consecutive sentences share the same length? If so, vary one.
- Is present tense reserved for the high-heat beats only, not sustained across a whole quiet chapter? (fatigue risk past ~40k words if sustained)
- Does the try-fail ladder shift pressure mode beat to beat (physical → social → moral), not just repeat "danger→danger"?
- In a won fight, can you point to a line where the protagonist is genuinely losing or improvising out of desperation? A dominant, unmarked win reads as power-fantasy.

## Kill list
- All-short-sentence fragment-bursts sustained past one beat — exhausting, reads as "trying too hard," not impact.
- All-long sentences with no short brake on the blow — no felt impact.
- Exhaustive blow-by-blow cataloguing of a single exchange (the Abercrombie flaw: repeated wound description past the point of new information).
- "He hit me, I hit him, he hit me again" — repetition without a turn.
- Crowd-awe triumph ("everyone watched in awe," a chant, a hero-monologue) — reject the reverent room.
- Injury that vanishes or is fully healed off-page with no trace in behavior.
- A bond stated ("she knew he was her best friend") instead of paid for in a costly act.
- Motivational-poster interiority ("I would never give up, because I was stronger than he could imagine").

## Micro-examples
**Fragment-burst on impact**
- Before: "The punch landed hard and it really hurt as he stumbled backward, dazed and in a lot of pain."
- After: "The fist caught his jaw. White noise. His knees turned to water. He was already falling."

**Long-short braking**
- Before: "He swung. I dodged. He swung again. I ducked. He kept swinging."
- After: "He cocked the axe back and stepped through with his whole weight behind it, certain this was the one that ended me — and I was already inside his arms. Too close to swing."
