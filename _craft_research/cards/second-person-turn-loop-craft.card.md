# Card — second-person-turn-loop-craft
Applies to every beat's ending/handback in a freeform second-person run (no menus) — governs where you stop, what you compress, and how the world resists the reader.

## Tests
- Does the beat end on a wanted outcome still in doubt (blackjack: risk rising, not settled)? If it ends after the dust settles, cut earlier and hand back sooner.
- Is this the emotional climax/revelation/ambush? If yes, do not enumerate options or narrate a tidy resolution — force a single inevitable beat or leave the moment open for the reader's own free reaction.
- Within a long beat, is there at least one internal scene-break or micro-beat that hands attention back before the final handoff? A solid wall of prose with no catch-and-throw fails.
- Does anything between the last reader action and the next require play? If not, compress it to a sentence ("time through action, not chronology") instead of rendering it.
- If the reader's action fails or is refused, does the outcome create a new problem/path (succeed at a cost, complication, raised stakes)? "Nothing happens" fails.
- Did the world just say yes because the reader wanted it to? If a request is granted with no cost or resistance, that's sycophancy — make it earn the yes, or have the world say no for an in-fiction reason.
- If this beat implies a future consequence ("she'll remember this"), is there a concrete later beat where it will actually fire? Don't imply what won't be discharged.
- Could an attentive reader have seen real danger coming before it lands? Unforeshadowed harm reads as ambush, not consequence — foreshadow first or make it survivable.
- Before this beat ships, does it hold against established facts (deaths, debts, who-knows-what)? Treat the vault/log as the record of truth over recalled context.

## Kill list
- Menu-style option enumeration at a climax, revelation, or ambush.
- A beat that resolves the tension cleanly and *then* asks what's next — resolve-then-prompt kills the cusp.
- "You fail. Nothing happens." — any dead-end failure with no redirect.
- Granting an implausible reader request purely to please; agreeable, cost-free yeses.
- Beat-by-beat rendering of dead travel/waiting time with no decision in it.
- A flagged future consequence that quietly never returns.
- Killing/harming the reader-character with zero prior hint of the danger.
- Trusting recalled "memory" of canon over the written record when they conflict.

## Micro-examples
**Want-at-risk handback (blackjack)**
- Before: The heist resolves cleanly — vault open, alarm silenced — then: "What do you do next?"
- After: End instead with the safe open, alarm wire live in your hand, footsteps starting on the stair — control passes back mid-risk, not after.

**Fail forward, not dead end**
- Before: "You try the lock. It doesn't give."
- After: "The lock holds — and the pick snaps off inside it. Now it's jammed, and boots are coming down the corridor."

**Sycophancy resistance**
- Before: Reader declares an absurd feat; narrator obliges, tension collapses.
- After: The world resists with a concrete in-fiction reason ("the rope's twenty feet short — wanting it longer doesn't make it so") and the attempt costs something before it can succeed.

### Digest — second-person-turn-loop-craft
When: every beat ending/handback in a freeform second-person run.
- At a climax, revelation, or ambush: no option menus — one inevitable beat, or leave the moment open.
- A failed action creates a new problem or path (cost, complication, raised stakes) — never "nothing happens."
- No yes just because the reader wants it: the yes is earned or the no has an in-fiction reason.
- Check the beat against the written record before shipping.
KILL: menus at a climax; dead-end failures; cost-free yeses; rendered dead time; flagged consequences that never fire; unforeshadowed lethal harm.
