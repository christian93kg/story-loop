# Card — foreshadowing-and-payoff
Applies when planting a twist, writing toward one, or paying one off. Runs off the story's `plot_twists.md` — each twist entry names its **plant beat** and **intended discharge beat**. Extends [[second-person-turn-loop-craft]]'s discharge rule ("a flagged consequence must fire") to branch-aware form.

## Tests
- Fair-play audit: list every fact used at the reveal; each must have an earlier on-page appearance accessible to the reader.
- Reread test: after the twist, at least two prior scenes carry a second, intended meaning verifiable against the text. Fewer = surprise, not recontextualization — decide which you meant.
- No-flag test: delete any sentence that tells the reader a detail will matter. If the plant can't survive without the flag, it isn't planted yet.
- Misjudgment allowed, misstatement never: the narrator may be wrong, but never asserts a fact the text later proves false.
- Orphaned-setup query: for every plant, is there a reachable path where it's set but never pays? If a plant is visible on a route where its payoff can't fire, scope the plant down to the routes that pay it.
- A payoff more than a chapter after its plant must echo the plant — re-cite it — or expect "where did that come from?"
- The narrator uses loaded terms and facts flatly before the reveal; any pre-reveal significance signal is telegraphing.
- The real clue sits among decoys; if there are no decoys, the plant is naked — fail.
- Delayed disclosure (reader behind the protagonist) is legal **iff** the fair-play audit passes — this is the condition that reconciles a shock reveal with the turn-loop anti-ambush rule.
- Suspense vs. surprise: if tension depends on the reader not knowing, ask whether telling them (Hitchcock's bomb under the table) would buy more — it usually does.

## Kill list
- "Little did I know" — any sentence announcing that a detail will matter
- The lying narrator (selective omission from true statements only)
- Withholding basic who/what/where so confusion impersonates mystery
- Unseeded shock twists; coincidence-driven reveals
- Prophecy/dream telegraphing
- Orphaned setups; plants visible on routes where the payoff can't fire
- Flagged consequences that never fire (see [[second-person-turn-loop-craft]])

## Micro-examples
The buried act (Ackroyd) —
Before: "I left the study." (The reveal later depends on an act the text never held.)
After: "I did what little had to be done… and closed the door behind me." — the act is inside the sentence, truthful, framed as trivial.

The casual mention (Rebecca) —
Before: "Little did I know how important that remark would prove."
After: the load-bearing fact dropped as end-of-scene gossip ("They say he can't get over his wife's death—"), no narration pointing at it.

Normalized vocabulary (Never Let Me Go) —
Before: "What did 'donor' really mean? I would find out."
After: the narrator uses "carer," "donor," "complete" flatly, as unremarkable vocabulary, from the first page.
