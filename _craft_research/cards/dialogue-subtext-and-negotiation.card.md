# Card — dialogue-subtext-and-negotiation
Applies to any conversation that matters — negotiation, interrogation, probing, domestic friction — in the register where the protagonist initiates, probes, and needles rather than receiving NPC talk.

## Tests
- Can you state each party's unspoken want in one sentence, without either character having said it aloud? If not, the scene isn't dramatic yet.
- Is the spoken line different from the unsaid sentence in the margin? Identical = on-the-nose.
- Does the protagonist advance by asking rather than asserting? Mark every line Q or A — the watchful PC asks at least as many questions as they answer.
- Is every fact spoken because a character is trying to win something with it *now*? Free facts fail.
- Does the listener concede, threaten, or reveal something to earn each significant fact?
- Does at least one reply refuse the question in a way that tells the reader more than an answer would?
- Can the scene's real subject be removed as a named noun and still be unmistakable — displaced onto a concrete object or an absent third party?
- Is each unit of white space a specific move — ellipsis (search), pause (shift), silence (rupture), cut-off (dominance), trail-off (evasion)?
- Does someone leave the scene having conceded, lost, or failed to resolve something? No clean exits.
- A gradient-flip line (the moment power reverses) is plot mechanics, not wit — it must change the scene's direction, never audition for the reader's admiration ([[grounded-sf-and-noir-menace]]: shorter and flatter under pressure, never wittier).

## Kill list
- "As you know" — if both characters already know it, the line exists only for the reader; cut or re-route
- On-the-nose lines that name the emotion or motive
- Bald exposition; backstory paragraphs (deliver history as present behavior — see [[grounded-sf-and-noir-menace]])
- NPC-as-lore-dispenser — the want "to give the player information" is the failure state
- The tidy reconciliation exit ("You're right, I'm sorry, let's put it behind us." / "Agreed.")
- Free facts — information surrendered without cost

## Micro-examples
Before: "As you know, we both trained under Colonel Reyes."
After: "Reyes taught you that grip. He taught me how to break it."

Question as move — the PC drives via a trap-question instead of asserting "I think you're lying":
"Tuesday. You said you were home." / "I was." / "Then whose jacket is drying by the depot stove?"

The want pursued, never stated:
"You slept fine last night?" / "…Why." / "No reason. You just look like a man who's decided something."
