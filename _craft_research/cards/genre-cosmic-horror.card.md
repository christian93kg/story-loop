# Card — genre-cosmic-horror
Applies whenever a beat aims for dread, the uncanny, or an unexplained/cosmic threat — haunted spaces, weird entities, wrongness that should not be resolved.

## Tests
- Does the paragraph name or measure the entity's properties (size, count, anatomy)? Catalogued = disclosure, not dread — fail.
- Is the vast/inhuman thing implied by a part, an aftermath, or a human's smallness, rather than shown whole?
- Is each anomaly individually explicable on its own, with only the accumulated series undeniable? One beat that forces the supernatural conclusion outright fails.
- Have you declined at least one resolution the reader expects by the end of the beat?
- Does the diction stay calm while the content is horrific (flat delivery), rather than matching tone to weight?
- Is there an ordinary in-world euphemism for the atrocity that characters use without comment, instead of naming it directly?
- Circle every adjective doing dread's work (eldritch/indescribable/ancient/wrong) — can the sentence still land with zero of them?
- Does the off detail sit in a sequence of otherwise ordinary/likable items, so its wrongness is the only signal?

## Kill list
- The eldritch/indescribable/cyclopean/ancient adjective register standing in for a chosen detail
- Named, measured, or anatomized monster/threat — full description substituting for a hint
- Narrator stating the threat's rules, origin, or mechanism
- A panicked or alarmed narrator whose stated fear outruns and caps what the reader infers
- Any single anomaly that, alone, forces a supernatural reading (should be deniable in isolation)
- Ending the beat with every open question answered
- Gory/graphic detail used as the "wrong" detail instead of a banal object placed out of true
- Tone/diction that matches the content's horror (screaming, sobbing prose) instead of contradicting it

## Micro-examples
Hint, not catalogue:
Before: "The creature was forty feet of chitinous limbs, seven eyes, and a maw of recurved teeth dripping black ichor."
After: "The tide had gone out further than it should have, and kept going, and from somewhere past the edge of what the light reached, the water was still moving toward us."

Wrong word, flatly delivered:
Before: "'They're all dead,' she sobbed in anguish."
After: "'They're all dead,' she said, and offered me more tea."

Decoded vocabulary (private euphemism):
Before: "The facility killed the subjects once their usefulness ended."
After: "When a subject was no longer indicated, it was sent down to Recovery. Nobody came back from Recovery, but that was just what it was called."

### Digest — genre-cosmic-horror
When: dread, the uncanny, any unexplained or cosmic threat.
- Imply the vast thing by a part, an aftermath, or a human's smallness — never named, measured, or anatomized.
- Each anomaly stays individually explicable; only the accumulated series is undeniable.
- The wrong detail arrives late, after reassuring material, inside an otherwise ordinary sequence; the body reacts before the mind recognizes.
- The reader grasps the horror one beat before the narrator could name it.
- Decline at least one expected resolution per beat; atrocities wear ordinary in-world euphemisms, used without comment.
KILL: eldritch/indescribable/ancient adjectives; catalogued monsters; stated rules or origins; panicked narrator; gore standing in for wrongness; all questions answered.
