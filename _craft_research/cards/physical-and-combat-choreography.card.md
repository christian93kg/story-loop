# Card — physical-and-combat-choreography
When: any beat with melee/grappling combat, lethal survival violence, or a non-fighter fleeing, hiding, or absorbing a beating.

## Tests
- Can the reader name one fixed object (rope, doorway, knife on counter, alley mouth) this beat's action is happening relative to? If not, add one.
- Across a long fight, does at least one tracked resource (breath, grip, legs) visibly deplete between exchanges? No depletion = power fantasy, add a clock.
- Is every grappling position rendered in plain spatial words AND a felt pressure (weight, leverage)? Named technique with no translation fails.
- Do control beats (pinned, closing options) read slow/static and scrambles (contested, opening) read fast/plural? Same texture for both fails.
- Does the kill/decisive blow get a triumphant or stylish beat? Strip to plain statement and add residue (the body's, the POV's) instead.
- Is an escape a sequence of terrain-and-stamina decisions, or just running? Add a constraint (line of sight, a gap, a clock) and a choice.
- During hiding, is the threat actively, methodically searching, with one specific small sound at stake? POV waiting safely fails.
- Has the opponent demonstrated — not been described as having — one capability the POV has no answer for, before the decisive exchange?

## Kill list
- Cataloguing every micro-movement or wound (the "blur of neverending chaos"); one dominant image per beat only.
- Grappling jargon (mount, guard, kimura, sprawl) left untranslated into plain words + felt pressure.
- Precise measurements/coordinates ("three feet," "two meters") — use relative position ("within reach," "behind you").
- Telegraphing the winner through narrator tone or confidence; withhold instead (McCarthy/Abercrombie flat affect).
- Stopping time for a slow-motion set-piece; keep prose moving at the speed of the body's panic.
- "Noisy hider" (character conveniently clumsy) and "selective car failure" (plot-convenient mechanical breakdown) as escape contrivances.
- A non-combatant's beating written as pure passive reception — no triage, no choice of what to protect, no reading the attacker.
- A beat that opens cold on a new cue without first paying off the reaction to the reader's last choice.

## Micro-examples
**Grappling — name + felt pressure, not jargon:**
Before: "He passed your guard and established side control, then worked toward mount."
After: "He slides his chest over yours and pins it, his weight stacked across your sternum so each breath comes shorter. Your arms are running out of places to push."

**Hiding — methodical threat, not a lull:**
Before: "You hid behind the crates and waited until they were gone, then came out."
After: "Footsteps. Unhurried, searching — pausing at each gap. One stops level with you. You think about the loose can by your knee and exactly how not to touch it."

### Digest — physical-and-combat-choreography
When: melee/grappling, survival violence, fleeing, hiding, taking a beating.
- Anchor action to one fixed object (doorway, rope, alley mouth); one tracked resource (breath, grip, legs) visibly depletes.
- Grappling in plain spatial words plus felt pressure (weight, leverage) — never untranslated jargon.
- Control beats read slow/static, scrambles fast/plural; the decisive blow is a plain statement plus residue, never style.
- One physical action per sentence; open by paying off the reader's last choice before the next cue.
- The opponent demonstrates one capability the POV can't answer before the decisive exchange.
KILL: micro-movement catalogues; slow-motion set-pieces; precise measurements; telegraphed winners; noisy-hider contrivances; passive beatings.
