"Nineteen minutes," you say. "And you've a building to find."

Isla eats one of the quarters, standing, and gets the folder into her bag one-handed.

"Half seven," she says. "The Annex. The gallery's free — you don't sign anything to sit in it." The bag goes onto her shoulder. She stops with it there. "And I'm not asking again. The lab sits four degrees under the corridor and Voss never says why. The steel on the benches is polished. The floor underneath is not. Third from the front, left side, is yours.

It's laid out when you get there. Both halves — scales trued, burner set, your mortar squared to the bench edge at the same distance as hers.

The girl doing it doesn't look up. Sleeves turned to the elbow, one fold each. A notebook by her left hand, red tab a third of the way in.

"Wren," she says.

"Sable."

"I know."

You look at your half of the bench. "Why?"

"Because it was going to bother me." She doesn't say which part.

Voss wants a stabilizing base by the hour. He says it once, from the front, then goes off down the row to be delighted at people.

She measures level, checks it, checks it again. You measure once. Batch three went in at six and has another two hours in it.

"Do you know why we're paired?"

"No. I asked the desk last night, then my advisor, who asked him." Her chin goes an inch toward the front of the room. "He said it was an interesting question."

"And you left it there."

"I left it there for now."

He comes down the row a few minutes later and stops at your bench. He looks at your flask, then at hers, then at neither of you.

"Good," he says, and moves on.

Your base comes to colour before hers. She looks at it, then at the clock, and writes something in the notebook that takes her four words.

The second time she looks at your hands instead of your flask, you ask her what she's counting.

"Stir rate." She doesn't stop working. "Yours doesn't drift. Most people's go off by the third minute."

"And the first time?"

She lets that one alone. She adds a half-measure to her own flask that her own flask does not need.

"You're in Marsh's book."

"For what."

"Trial Night. Sable, first-year. Fourteen to one." She sets her spoon across the rim, level. "Entered."

You have not entered.

"He took money on it before lecture," she says. "In front of people."

Her hands have stopped moving. "Are you going?"
