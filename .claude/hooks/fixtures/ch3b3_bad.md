"Nineteen minutes," you say. "And Structural Chem's in a building you found out about this morning. Eat something."

Isla doesn't answer straight off. Then she takes one of the quarters and eats it, which isn't agreement so much as the nearest thing she'll hand over before nine.

"Half seven," she says, standing, sweeping the folder into her bag in the same motion. "The Annex. The gallery's free — you don't sign anything to sit in it." The bag goes onto her shoulder. She stops with it there. "And I'm not asking again. About last night, or the—" Her hand does something that takes in the table, last night, all of it. "You'll say it when you've decided to say it. So there's no sense in me— yeah. Half seven."

She goes. You finish the coffee, because it's there.

---

The lab sits two floors below the common room and colder than the corridor that leads to it, which is a choice somebody made and nobody has undone in forty years. Twelve benches in pairs, steel fittings kept to a shine that costs someone their evenings. Third from the front, left side, is yours.

It's laid out when you get there. Not her half — both halves. Scales trued, burner set, mortar squared to the bench edge on your side exactly as it is on hers, mirrored, as though the station were built for one person with two sets of hands.

The girl doing it doesn't look up. Sleeves rolled to the elbow, one even turn each. A notebook by her left hand with a red tab a third of the way in.

"Wren." She squares the last thing and steps back from it. "I set yours out because I got here first and it would have bothered me otherwise. Not a favor."

"Sable."

"I know."

Voss wants a stabilizing base by the hour. He says it once, from the front, in the voice of a man who has said it to eleven other intakes and would rather be at breakfast, and then he moves off down the row to be delighted at somebody.

You work. She works. She measures the way people do who were taught to — level, checked, checked again — and you measure the way you do, which is once. Twice through, she looks at your hand instead of your flask, and both times she goes back to her own without saying anything about it, which is more restraint than most people manage before lunch.

Halfway into the second reduction she speaks without stopping what her hands are doing.

"You're in the book. Marsh's." She trues her scale a hair it doesn't need. "Trial Night. Sable, first-year — fourteen to one. He's got you down as entering, and he's taken money on it already, which is why I'm mentioning it rather than minding my own."

You have not entered.

"Are you going?" she says. Her hands have stopped. It's the first time they have.
